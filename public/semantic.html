How it thinks

Not a folder of files.
A memory that understands.

Ask the way you think, in any language you like. Second Brain finds what you meant, even when you wrote it in other words. Try it on this small sample Brain of 29 notes.

Exact words–

Finds a note only if it contains the very words you typed, spelled the same way.

        Under the hood
        
          
        Three layers of knowing, in one file on your computer
        
          
        Your Brain is a single SQLite database in your user folder. Every item lives there with three ways to be found,
            next to its original file and the history of every change.
        
        
        

        
        
          
        
            1
            
        Words
        
            
        A full-text index ranks notes and document pages by the words they hold (BM25). A word in the title counts four
              times as much as in the body. Exact names, numbers and codes are found here.
        
            notes_fts · chunks_fts
          
        
          
        
            2
            
        Meaning
        
            
        Every note, and every page of every document cut into pieces of about 1,200 characters, gets a vector of 1,536
              numbers that captures what it means. Same idea in other words, even in another language: close vectors.
        
            note_embeddings · source_chunks
          
        
          
        
            3
            
        Links
        
            
        Your own links, the ones the Archivist adds when it files, and the “same idea” pairs it computes. You keep a
              suggestion or dismiss it, and the Brain remembers your answer.
        
            links · note_related · related_choices
          
        
          
        
            
        Around them: places (PARA), types and fields (a recipe has servings), tags, originals of your files,
              restore points and a timeline of every change. spaces · item_types · sources · restore_points
        
          
        
        
        

        
        
          
        How a question is answered
        
          
          
            
        1. Meaning first. The question becomes a vector; the closest notes above 0.25 come back, best first.
          2. 
            
        2. Words complete it. Keyword hits not already found fill the list: an exact name is never lost.
          3. 
            
        3. Documents next. For “what does the quote say?”, the same two searches run on document pieces, and the
              answer cites the page.
          4. 
            
        4. Places when you name one. “What's in my Inbox?” lists the place itself, newest first.

        AI-native
        
          
        The right model for each job
        
          
        Second Brain is not one chatbot. Each job goes to the model that does it best: a fast one to talk, a reasoning
            one for the weekly review, a transcriber for your voice, an embeddings model for meaning, and your own computer
            for what it can do alone. Pick a way in to see the route.

        Measured, not guessed
        Chat with tools moved to gpt-6-sol after replaying the owner's real
            failures on a throwaway Brain: 12 out of 12, against 11 for gpt-4o. Filing: 10 out of 10 test captures in 1.45 s.
        
          
        Never stuck
        Every model is a setting. When your OpenAI project lacks one, the job falls back
            (to gpt-4o or gpt-4o-mini) instead of failing.
        
          
        Local where it can be
        PDF text, scanned pages (Windows OCR or Tesseract), keyword search, the
            graph and your files stay on your computer. Local AI models are on the roadmap.
        
        
        
      
        
    
        

    
    
        
      
        
        
        
          
        Agents
        
          
        An AI that searches, instead of reading everything
        
          
        A Brain grows to thousands of items; no model should swallow it whole. The agents use tools, like you would use
            a search engine: search, open the few items that matter, cite them, act only through tools you can undo.

        Guardrails, in the code
        
              
          
                
        • At most 8 tool rounds per reply.
          • 
                
        • No claim without a tool. A reply that says “done” when no tool ran is caught and corrected.
          • 
                
        • Not sure? It asks. Below a confidence of 0.6, the item waits in the Inbox with a question.
          • 
                
        • Your clipboard is read only when you mention it.
          • 
                
        • Every change leaves a receipt, an Undo and a restore point.

        Over MCP
        
          
        One memory for every agent you use
        
          
        Claude Code, Codex or Claude Desktop plug into the Brain over MCP. They search it by meaning; what they learn comes
            back as proposals the Archivist files with your rules. What one agent learns, the next one knows.
        
        
        

        
        
          
          
        
            
          
              
        1. Ask. A Claude Code session in Agent Manager calls search: “what do we know about the quote?”
          2. 
              
        2. Answer. The Brain searches by meaning and words, and sends three short hits with their ids.
          3. 
              
        3. Propose. The session learned something; it calls propose_item. Nothing is written yet.
          4. 
              
        4. File. The Archivist checks it with your rules, files it, links it, and answers with a receipt.
          5. 
              
        5. Remember. Tomorrow, a Codex session asks a related question and finds it.
          6. 
            
        
            
        
          
        
        
        

        
        
          
        readSearch, open items, list places, find paths.
        
          
        propose itemsRead, plus propose new items to the Archivist.
        
          
        propose changesAlso propose changes to existing items. Still never a direct write.
        
        
        
        
        Each agent has its own token and level, and the Brain listens on your computer only. Levels
          are permissions inside the app, not network security: read the AI notice.
        
      
        
    
        

    
    
        
      
        
        
        What it takes, honestly
        
        
          
          
        • Meaning needs an OpenAI key. Without one, everything still works with words: search, filing by hand, the graph.
          • 
          
        • What you send to a model leaves your computer. The text of a note to make its vector, your question to answer
            it. Your Brain itself, its files and its history stay in your user folder.
          • 
          
        • Built for one person on one computer. It is a lab, not a server: don't put it on the internet.
