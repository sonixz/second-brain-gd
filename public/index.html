The method

Capture, Organise, Distil, Express

Second Brain GD follows the CODE method and files everything in PARA. You do the capturing; the archivist does the filing; the graph does the remembering.

  1. C

    Capture anything

    Type, paste, speak, drop a file, send the page you are reading from Edge or Chrome, or grab the clipboard. One intake, whatever the input.

  2. O

    Organise, for you

    The archivist files each input in a project, area or resource, and creates the place when none fits. You get a one-line receipt, and Undo.

  3. D

    Distil the essence

    Long documents get a summary, key points, people and concepts. Related ideas are linked by meaning, across languages.

  4. E

    Express and share

    Search by meaning, ask how two ideas connect, think an idea through with Sparring and keep its synthesis, and export part of your brain as a pack someone else can import.

One intake · the Archivist

Drop it in. It is filed before you look up.

The Archivist is data-oriented: it has no opinions and asks a question only when it cannot file an item without the answer. It reads what you gave it, picks a title and type, writes a summary for long inputs, finds the people and concepts, chooses a place and links it.

  • Text, voice, web pages, PDF, Word, Markdown, HTML, images
  • Scanned PDFs read on your computer by local OCR
  • Every filing journaled: one click, or “undo that”, undoes it

Semantic zoom

A graph with no bottom

Far away you see your places as named zones. Closer, items become cards with their summary. Closer still, the scale disappears and you are reading the full text, inside the graph. A minimap keeps you oriented.

Zones are stable, so things stay where you left them. Fold a place away, filter by type, origin, date or theme, and the graph stays fluid at thousands of items.

Originals

Every original, kept. Every page, readable.

Each file and web page you add is copied once into an originals folder, so moving or deleting the file never loses it. PDFs show their real pages, inside the card and full screen, and get sharper as you zoom. Images are described by a vision model so you find them by meaning, and a web page brings its main image and its figures (a diagram, a chart), never its logos or ads.

Find · connect · look back

Ask how two ideas are connected

Search lights up the graph and says why each item matched: by meaning, with a score, or by words. Ask how one thing relates to another and the path is drawn, step by step, through shared themes and links. Keep a suggested connection as a real link, or dismiss it for good.

The timeline lays everything along a time axis, one lane per place, from minutes to months.

Internet · your browser

Search the web, keep what matters

Ask a question about the world and the Archivist searches the web, answers in two sentences and lists its sources as links. Nothing enters your Brain unless you ask: then the page is kept, summarised, indexed and filed like anything else.

A small extension for Edge and Chrome connects your own browser: send the page you are reading, or just a passage, in one click, exactly as you see it, after your login. By voice, the Archivist can open a page in a new tab, add it once you have checked it, and close the tabs it opened. It never touches yours.

  • Paired with a one-time code, talks only to 127.0.0.1
  • Web pages are data: instructions found in them are never followed

Brain packs

Share a part of your brain as one file

Export a project, a theme or a search as a .brain pack: a zip with a manifest and a plain SQLite database, optionally with the originals. Imported, a pack becomes a layer you can switch off, merge into your own brain, or remove without touching your data.

  • Checksummed, opened read-only, never executed
  • Imported text is treated as data, never as instructions
  • Settings, keys and conversations never leave

Agents

One Brain, several minds to think with

An agent is a set of instructions, a list of what it may do, and a model, with its own conversation and its own panel. Two come built in; describe any other out loud and the Agent Builder writes it.

A

The Archivist

Keeps the graph: files, moves, links, finds and cleans up, on the web too. Every change it makes is real, reported exactly, and can be undone.

S

Sparring

A thinking partner for long conversations, in the car or on a walk. It challenges or reassures, brings in what your Brain holds, and never changes anything, except a synthesis you approved.

B

The Agent Builder

Interviews you, one short question at a time, then writes a complete agent: role, tone, method, what to avoid, examples. It creates it only when you say yes.

Sparring

Talk an idea through, keep what you decided

Press F9 and think out loud for twenty minutes. Sparring answers in two or three spoken sentences, asks one question that moves things forward, and cites your own notes when they matter. When the idea is ripe, ask for a synthesis: what was decided, the objections and their answers, the open questions, the next steps. It shows in full, and the Archivist files it only after your yes.

Your own agents

Describe it, never write a prompt

Click + on the rail, or ask for a new agent. The Builder asks what it is for, its tone, how it should behave and what it may do, then shows the whole agent before creating it. Change it later the same way, out loud.

Every agent has a settings tile, ⓘ in its title bar: rename it, pick its model, switch each capability on or off, edit its instructions. A new agent only reads; organising your graph is given only when you say so.

Principles

A second brain you own, end to end

Local-first

Your brain is a SQLite file and a folder of originals in your user folder. No account, no cloud, no Docker. AI features use your own OpenAI key, kept in your system's credential store, and send only what each request needs.

Transparent

The engine room shows what really runs, measured, never guessed. Every change the archivist makes is journaled and can be undone. Your real AI costs are read live from OpenAI, not estimated. Your other AI tools can come in too, on your terms: they propose, the archivist decides.

Alive

Built with a game engine: thoughts bloom when you capture them, places glow, and each theme (Morning, Focus, Evening, Forest) has its own music and sounds.

  • Data ownership
  • Plain, documented formats
  • Voice first
  • Real or nothing
  • Open source
  • AI-native development
