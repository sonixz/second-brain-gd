SECOND BRAIN GD

Your knowledge. Your system. Your control.

A personal knowledge environment that runs on your own computer, with an assistant who briefs you each morning, answers from your own notes and files every thought you type or say. Built with a game engine, so it feels alive.

Vision

A second brain you own, end to end

Local-first

Your notes and your database live in your user folder, on your disk. No account and no Docker. AI features use your own OpenAI key, stored in your system's credential store, and send only what each request needs.

Immersive

Built with a game engine. Your ideas form a living constellation, thoughts bloom when you capture them, and each theme (Morning, Focus, Evening) has its own music and sounds.

Transparent

The engine room shows what really runs, with real measurements only. Everything the assistant changes is journaled and can be undone, and every AI call shows its estimated cost.

How it works

From clone to a running system in a few clicks

  1. 1

    Open the project in Godot

    Run it. The welcome screen reads the install state from disk.

  2. 2

    Press Setup

    Godot finds a compatible Python (3.12 or 3.13) and starts the bootstrap without freezing the interface.

  3. 3

    Watch the real installation

    Virtual environment, hash-locked dependencies, SQLite migrations. Each step streams structured events, progress and logs.

  4. 4

    Talk to your brain

    Type or speak a thought: it blooms in the graph and the assistant files it in the right project. Each morning she reads you a briefing. Ask her anything and she answers from your notes.

Open-source philosophy

Readable, inspectable, yours to change

Second Brain GD is developed in the open spirit: plain formats, a documented event protocol, and an engine you can read in an afternoon. Nothing is hidden behind a service. Metrics that cannot be measured say “Not available” instead of guessing.

The repository is private while the foundations settle. The license will be chosen before the first public release.

Development journal

Built in loops

Loop 14 November 3, 2026

Search and connect

Search now lights up the graph and lists each hit with why it matched: by meaning, or by words. Ask « comment ma routine du matin est reliée aux risques d'Atlas ? » and the path appears, bright, through a shared theme and a weekly review, and the archivist says it in one sentence. Related-idea suggestions can be kept as real links or dismissed for good, and a double-click lets you edit an item right on its card.

Loop 13 November 3, 2026

An organised graph

Every place now has its own zone: the Inbox in the middle, projects, areas and resources around it, each with its items gathered close. Double-click a place, or say « replie Recettes », and its items fold into it. A filter bar shows only what you ask for: articles, things you spoke, what came in this week, a word or a theme. « Montre seulement les articles sur l'IA » works by voice, and the graph tells you how many items are showing.

Loop 12 November 3, 2026

The originals, and a page you can zoom into

The brain now keeps every file and web page you give it, in its own originals folder, once, even if you later move or delete the file. Zoom into a PDF in the graph and its real first page appears in the card. Open it and the pages fill the screen, getting sharper as you zoom in. « Page suivante », « ouvre l'original »: it works by voice too. Images are welcome as well; a vision model describes them so you can find them by meaning.

Loop 7b November 3, 2026

The first real conversation

With the live voice model enabled, the first real exchange went through: « ouvre la vue des captures », the view opened, and she answered aloud, for a third of a cent. One surprise on the way: the transcription model the app used elsewhere is not accepted inside a live session, so the conversation now uses another one.

Loop 11 November 3, 2026

Brain packs

Say « exporte le projet Atlas » and the project leaves as one .brain file: its items, places, themes, links, summaries and meaning vectors, never your keys, settings or conversations. Drop someone's pack on the window and it arrives as a layer, marked with a dotted ring in the graph. Switch the layer off, merge it into your own brain, or remove it, by voice too. Identical items are skipped, and imported text is treated as data, never as instructions.

Loop 10 November 3, 2026

The graph first, and a zoom with no bottom

The brain is the home screen again. Today, Projects and Agents step aside as optional modules. Zoom out and each place becomes a named cluster. Zoom in and items turn into cards with their place, themes and summary. Keep going and the full text is right there, readable inside the item. A minimap keeps you oriented, and « zoome sur… » or « vue d'ensemble » does it all by voice.

Loop 9 November 3, 2026

One intake, and an archivist

Type a thought, paste a link, drop a PDF, speak, press Ctrl+Shift+V, or click « Envoyer au Brain » in your browser: everything goes through the same funnel. The archivist reads it, notes the people, ideas and dates, writes a summary for long pieces, and files it. On an empty brain, it creates the places it needs: a sourdough recipe started « Resources › Recettes », a real article on PARA started « Resources › Organisation de l'information ». It gives no opinions, asks only when it must, and every filing can be undone. A new install now starts almost empty, with a short guide.

Loop 8 November 3, 2026

Real costs, and a livelier voice

The dashboard shows what OpenAI actually bills: this month, today, a 30-day chart by model, tokens and line items, read live from OpenAI with a read-only key and stored nowhere. The spoken voice is faster (adjustable, without turning squeaky), answers given by voice are shorter, and every answer has a replay button: « relis-moi ça » reads it again.

Loop 7b November 3, 2026

A real conversation, almost

Press F9 and talk: speech in, speech out, with the same tools as the text assistant, and a small pill showing what this conversation costs so far. It is built and tested end to end against a faithful simulation; the first conversation with OpenAI's live voice model waits for that model to be enabled on the account.

Loop 7a November 3, 2026

Hands-free

Hold F8, speak, let go. « Montre-moi le projet Atlas », « ouvre ma note sur ma routine du soir », « mets le thème du soir pis baisse le son », « on fait la revue de la semaine », « annule ça »: the assistant answers aloud and the interface follows. No clicks needed. A small indicator shows exactly when the microphone is listening.

Loop 6c November 3, 2026

The agent room

Agents arrive, on by default. Setup downloads OpenAI's Codex agent, checks its fingerprint, and needs no administrator rights and no extra software. Add a project folder, start an agent, and watch it read, plan and propose. Every file it wants to change and every command it wants to run appears as a card: approve once, approve for the session, or decline. Nothing outside your chosen folders, nothing without your yes.

Since then it has run for real, with OpenAI's actual agent on Linux and on Windows, without administrator rights. Asked to create a file and read another, it asked permission for every step. Declined, it touched nothing. Approved, it finished in about eleven seconds.

Loop 6b November 3, 2026

A weekly review that thinks

The weekly review now runs on a reasoning model. It reads your week the way a coach would: the client who wants to push the demo back, the supplier call that is late. It connects the two, proposes one concrete next step per project, and cites your notes. If your own OpenAI account does not offer that model, the review quietly uses another one and tells you once.

Loop 5b November 3, 2026

A memory that understands meaning

Every note is now indexed by meaning, quietly in the background. Ask « c'est quoi mes habitudes pour mieux dormir? » and the assistant finds your English note “Bedtime routine”, though no word matches. Notes about the same idea now find each other: they are joined in the graph by a warm dashed line, and listed as related ideas when you open one. Measured on real notes: three true pairs found, no false ones.

Loop 6a November 2, 2026

Project mode

Open a project and the assistant works inside it: she knows its goal, due date and next actions, searches the project first, and follows the project's own instructions. Tell a client project “English-speaking client, contact Mark Chen” and the follow-up email comes out in English, addressed to Mark.

Loop 5 November 2, 2026

Sources: files and links

Drop a PDF or a Word file on the window, or paste a link: the brain reads it, writes a short summary card into your graph and indexes every passage. Ask the assistant and she answers from your documents, with the source cited.

Loop 4 November 2, 2026

Structure: areas, projects, today

Your life domains (family, health, home, vision at work) now hold projects with a goal, a due date and next actions. A Today view shows what is late, due and next, plus your lists. Say « faut acheter du lait pis du café » and it lands on the grocery list. Once a week, the assistant walks you through every project and asks the questions that move your vision forward.

Loop 3 November 2, 2026

An assistant who talks back

The assistant arrives. At the first opening of the day she reads a short briefing aloud: open tasks, what is new, an idea that has been sleeping. She answers from your notes and cites them, saves or moves notes when you ask, and files every capture in the right project. Everything she changes can be undone. You can type to her or speak to her.

Loop 2 November 2, 2026

Voice capture

Press the microphone and your words appear while you speak, then bloom as a note. Each user brings their own OpenAI key during onboarding. It is stored in the system's credential store and never by the interface. Measured on the real API: English transcribed without errors, first words in about a second.

Loop 1 November 2, 2026

Immersion and the knowledge core

The second brain itself arrives: a living graph where captured thoughts bloom, spaces for projects, [[links]], #tags, full-text search and a morning briefing. A soft blue Morning theme replaces the green, with Focus, Evening and Forest alongside. Each theme has generated music, ambience and interface sounds.

Loop 0 November 2, 2026

Foundation, bootstrap and System Console

The first increment works end to end: Godot finds Python, a standard-library bootstrap builds the environment, migrates SQLite and starts a local FastAPI service, and the System Console shows live, measured state. Tested headless on Linux (Python 3.12) and native Windows (Microsoft Store Python 3.13), including a fix for the Store's silent AppData redirection.
